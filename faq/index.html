before buying smart glasses.

Short answers first, then the important exceptions. Smart glasses span several product categories, so the correct answer often depends on whether the product is AI eyewear, a heads-up display, or a wearable monitor.

Do smart glasses need a phone?

Often, but not always—and some display glasses need a video source rather than a phone app.

Can smart glasses work without Wi-Fi?

Some functions can; cloud AI and online translation usually need connectivity.

Do all smart glasses have cameras?

No. Camera-free options are common in display and heads-up categories.

Can smart glasses use prescription lenses?

Many can, but integrated lenses, inserts and diopter adjustment are different solutions.

Are smart glasses good for gaming?

Display glasses can be, especially with compatible USB-C video output and high refresh rates.

AR glasses vs smart glasses

“Smart glasses” is the broader category; AR glasses specifically place digital visuals into your view.

Why GlassesDB answers by category

A Ray-Ban Meta-style camera-and-audio product, an Even G2-style heads-up display and an XREAL-style wearable monitor can all be called “smart glasses,” but they depend on different hardware, apps and connectivity. Category comes before the yes/no answer.
